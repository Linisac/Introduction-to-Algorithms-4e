\section{Maintaining the heap property}
\subsection*{Note}
\begin{enumerate}
%
\item We can argue the correctness of the procedure $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, i)$ on page 165 using an analogous method to stating a loop invariant and proving it holds. More precisely, we make the statement below for this procedure, a so-called \textbf{\textit{recursion correctness}}.
\begin{recurcorr}
After performing $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, i)$, the binary tree rooted at node $i$ becomes a max-heap, provided that the subtrees rooted at the children of node $i$ (if any) were already max-heaps themselves prior to executing $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, i)$.
\end{recurcorr}
Then, we prove the above statement by induction, which involves the \textbf{\textit{basis step}} and the \textbf{\textit{inductive step}} and, together with the \textbf{\textit{termination}} part (it guarantees the procedure always terminates), implies the correctness of the procedure.
\begin{proofinduct}
%
\Basis If node $i$ is a leaf, then the binary tree rooted at node $i$ is already a max-heap and executing $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, i)$ leaves this single-node max-heap unchanged.
%
\Induct Assume that node $i$ is not a leaf, that is, it has a left child and may have a right child, and that the subtree(s) rooted at the child(ren) are already max-heaps. If $\id{largest}$ is equal to $i$ at line 8 in the procedure $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, i)$, then the binary tree rooted at node $i$ is already a max-heap and no change is made by this procedure. Otherwise, line 9 exchanges $A[i]$ and $A[\id{largest}]$ so as to fix the violation(s) of max-heap property among $A[i]$, $A[\proc{Left}(i)]$ and $A[\proc{Right}(i)]$. Suppose that $\id{largest}$ is equal to $\proc{Left}(i)$ at line 8, then by the assumption that the subtree rooted at node $\proc{Left}(i)$ is a max-heap (which means that the subtree(s) rooted at the child(ren) of $\proc{Left}(i)$, if any, are max-heaps as well), performing $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, \id{largest})$ results in the subtree rooted at node $\proc{Left}(i)$ becoming a max-heap, by induction hypothesis, and consequently, the entire binary tree rooted at node $i$ becomes a max-heap because every node occurring in the subtree rooted at node $\proc{Left}(i)$ has value no greater than that of node $i$; the other case, where $\id{largest}$ is equal to $\proc{Right}(i)$, can be argued analogously and hence is omitted. Therefore, when $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, i)$ is done, the binary tree rooted at node $i$ is a max-heap.
%
\Term If node $i$ is a leaf, then $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, i)$ immediately terminates, without making any changes to $A$. Otherwise, the procedure may recursively call $\proc{Max-Heapify}(A, \id{largest})$ on line 3; the second argument in each recursive call gets larger and larger, and it eventually arrives at a node that is a leaf. Therefore, the procedure always terminates.
%
\end{proofinduct}

\begin{tabular}{cccc}

             & $\bullet$ & $\bullet$ &           \cr
             & $\bullet$ & $\bullet$ & $\bullet$ \cr\hline
$\therefore$ &           &           & $\bullet$ \cr
\end{tabular}
\cr
recursion correctness \cr
\end{tabular}
\hspace{5em}
\begin{tabular}{c}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
             & $\circ$ &         &           \cr
             &         & $\circ$ &           \cr
             &         &         & $\circ$   \cr\hline
$\therefore$ &         &         & $\bullet$ \cr
\end{tabular}
\cr
loop invariant \cr
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{remark}

\end{enumerate}

\item Let $Y$ be a Young tableau and let $k$ be a number. Observe that if $Y[i, j] < k$, then $Y[i, j'] < k$ for all $j' \leq j$, and that if $Y[i, j] > k$, then $Y[i', j] > k$ for all $i' \geq i$. Using these observations, the procedure below searches $Y$ for $k$.
\begin{codebox}
\Procname{$\proc{Search}(Y, k)$}
\li $i \gets 1$
\li $j \gets \attrib{Y}{col-size}$
\li \While ($i \leq \attrib{Y}{row-size}$ and $j \geq 1$) and $Y[i, j] \neq k$ \label{li:while-loop-start-for-search}
\li \Do
         \If $Y[i, j] < k$
\li      \Then
               $i \gets i + 1$
\li      \Else \Comment{$Y[i, j] > k$}
\li            $j \gets j - 1$
         \End
    \End \label{li:while-loop-end-for-search}
\li \If $i \leq \attrib{Y}{row-size}$ and $j \geq 1$ \Comment{$Y[i, j] \isequal k$}
\li \Then \Return $\const{true}$
\li \Else \Return $\const{false}$
    \End
\end{codebox}
Obviously, the \kw{while} loop in lines \ref{li:while-loop-start-for-search}--\ref{li:while-loop-end-for-search} is performed at most $m + n$ times, where $m \defas \attrib{Y}{row-size}$ and $n \defas \attrib{Y}{col-size}$.
\begin{remark}
The above procedure was inspired by the one given in \url{https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~ajl213/CLRS/Ch6.pdf}. Would it be more efficient to use binary search instead of linear search in the \kw{while} loop?
\end{remark}
